# Point Transformer V3: Simpler, Faster, Stronger
**Authors**: Xiaoyang Wu, Li Jiang, Peng-Shuai Wang, Zhijian Liu, Xihui Liu, Yu Qiao, Wanli Ouyang, Tong He, Hengshuang Zhao
**Year / Venue**: 2024, CVPR (arXiv comments field: "CVPR 2024, code available at Pointcept"). arXiv v1 15 Dec 2023, v2 25 Mar 2024.
**Cite key**: wu_2024_ptv3
**Source read**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10035 (comments field and metadata); https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.10035 (full text, v2). The fetch summariser could not read the binary PDF, so the text was extracted locally with pdftotext from the saved PDF.

## In one paragraph
Earlier point transformers (PTv1, PTv2) found each point's neighbours by kNN search and added a learned relative position encoding. Together these two steps cost 54% of PTv2's forward time (28% kNN and 26% relative position encoding), which kept the attention window tiny, 16 points. PTv3 gives up exact neighbourhoods. It sorts the points along space-filling curves (Z-order and Hilbert, plus transposed variants), so that neighbours in the sorted list are mostly close in space, and then cuts the sorted list into non-overlapping patches of 1024 points. Standard dot-product attention runs inside each patch, with the serialisation order changed between layers so that patches mix. A sparse convolution before each attention layer replaces the relative position encoding. The paper's argument is that scale (a bigger receptive field, more data, multi-dataset training) matters more than exact neighbour search.

## Input and output
- Input: a point cloud with coordinates (and colour or other features), turned into a sparse grid by grid sampling (grid size 0.02 for indoor and 0.05 for outdoor data, per the appendix). Each point gets a 64-bit serialisation code: batch index in the leading bits, space-filling-curve index of the grid cell in the trailing bits.
- Output: per-point features for semantic segmentation, instance segmentation (as a backbone for PointGroup) or detection (as a backbone for CenterPoint). The U-Net decoder returns to the full input resolution.

## Architecture
- Serialisation: points are sorted by their code. The paper uses four patterns, Z-order, transposed Z-order, Hilbert and transposed Hilbert. Only index mappings are stored; the points are not physically reordered.
- Patch attention: the sorted sequence is padded (by borrowing points from neighbouring patches) and split into non-overlapping patches of 1024 points. Attention is plain dot-product (flash attention is possible) inside each patch.
- Patch interaction: "Shift Order" cycles through the serialisation patterns between attention blocks; "Shuffle Order" randomises that cycle. The paper compares these against Shift Dilation and Shift Patch (Table 2); shuffle order with all four patterns is best (77.3 in their ablation, against 74.3 for a single pattern with no interaction scheme).
- Positional encoding: xCPE, a sparse convolution layer with a skip connection placed before the attention layer (Table 3: absolute 72.1, relative 75.9, xCPE 77.3 mIoU on ScanNet validation).
- Block: pre-norm, LayerNorm (the paper reports batch norm is still needed in the pooling layers), attention, MLP.
- Pooling: grid pooling (from PTv2), with grid size multipliers [x2, x2, x2, x2].
- Layout (Table 15): U-Net with four encoder and four decoder stages. Embedding depth 2, 32 channels. Encoder depths [2, 2, 6, 2], channels [64, 128, 256, 512], heads [4, 8, 16, 32], patch size 1024 in every stage. Decoder depths [1, 1, 1, 1], channels [64, 64, 128, 256], heads [4, 4, 8, 16]. MLP ratio 4, drop path 0.3.
- Size and speed: 46.2 M parameters (Table 1 and Table 11). Against PTv2 on nuScenes (Table 1): inference 44 ms and 1.2 G of memory, against 146 ms and 12.3 G; the paper states a 3.3 times speed-up and a 10.2 times memory reduction (the abstract says "3x" and "10x"). Against PTv2 on ScanNet (Table 11): inference 61 ms against 191 ms, with 5.2 G against 18.2 G.
- Receptive field: patch size 1024 against 16 or 24 in earlier work (Table 4: patch size 16 gives 75.0, 1024 gives 77.3, 4096 gives 77.1).

## Training data and labels
- Semantic segmentation on ScanNet v2, ScanNet200, S3DIS, nuScenes, SemanticKITTI and Waymo; instance segmentation with PointGroup; Waymo object detection. The paper states results on "over 20 downstream tasks".
- Loss for semantic segmentation (Tables 12 and 13): cross-entropy plus Lovasz loss, each with weight 1. Optimiser AdamW with a cosine schedule.
- Multi-dataset joint training ("PPT", Point Prompt Training) on ScanNet, S3DIS and Structured3D indoors, or nuScenes, SemanticKITTI and Waymo outdoors, is used for the stronger results.

## Headline results
- ScanNet validation mIoU (Table 5): 77.5 trained from scratch, 78.6 with multi-dataset pre-training, against PTv2 75.4 and MinkUNet 72.2.
- S3DIS Area 5 mIoU: 73.4 from scratch (Table 5, and 73.43 in Table 6); 6-fold mIoU 77.70 from scratch and 80.81 with pre-training (Table 6).
- Efficiency: 3.3 times faster and 10.2 times lower memory than PTv2 (nuScenes, Table 1), receptive field 16 to 1024 points.

## How it differs from our model
- It has: a hierarchy (grid pooling, four stages), attention inside 1024-point patches, four serialisation orders cycled across layers, sparse-convolution positional encoding, 46.2 M parameters, and scale-oriented training (multi-dataset).
- Ours has: exact kNN neighbourhoods (k = 16, computed once on xyz), a very small model (about 0.2 M parameters), no grid or voxel step, and every point is a native output.
- Because the patch size (1024) is already half or a quarter of an object with N = 2048 to 4096, attention here would be close to global within the object. The paper's own claim is that a large receptive field is the point.
- Voxel and grid scale: PTv3 grid-samples at 2 cm indoors and uses grid pooling and sparse convolution. A typical kitchen object is 10 to 30 cm, so it would need a millimetre-scale grid, and points that share a grid cell are treated together (this is my reading; the paper does not discuss single objects). The package also needs sparse-convolution and flash-attention dependencies.
- Benchmarks are multi-class softmax segmentation of scenes with up to 128,000 points per crop, and the paper's strength is scale. Ours is per-point sigmoid binary cross-entropy on simulated labels from a few thousand points per object; rankings may not transfer, and the paper itself says current point transformers show "over-capacity" for existing tasks.
- Plausible impact of switching for per-point heatmaps on single objects of 2k to 8k points: unlikely to pay off. The gains come from large scenes and large data. With a small simulated dataset, a 46.2 M parameter model risks overfitting and is hard to defend as a fair comparison without the same data scale.

## Likely supervisor questions
1. Q: Why not use the current state-of-the-art backbone? A: PTv3's improvements are about scale: wide receptive fields over scenes with many thousands of points and multi-dataset pre-training. Our inputs are single objects with 2k to 8k points, our model has about 0.2 M parameters, and our labels are simulation outcomes. PTv3 is a candidate for a final ablation, not a default.
2. Q: PTv3 gives up exact neighbours. Does it matter for you? A: For contact reasoning exact local neighbours probably matter, which is why we use kNN once on xyz. PTv3 argues that attention can compensate for imprecise neighbourhoods once the model is large and data is plentiful. We have not shown that holds at our scale.
3. Q: What does serialisation mean for rotation or pose? A: Space-filling curve order depends on the axes, so the neighbours in the sequence change with orientation. PTv3 uses four pattern variants and random rotation augmentation (about the z axis) to cope. Our kNN graph is rotation independent but the xyz input is not, so pose augmentation is a separate issue for both.
