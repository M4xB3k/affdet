## (A1) Learned stable placement prediction (learning-to-place, 6-DoF placement, rearrangement models)

### Takeaway
Learned placement models fall into three groups. (i) Per-object "which surface/plane does it rest on" predictors: Jiang et al. 2012, UOP-Net 2023, and a 2025 placeability metric. (ii) Generative 6-DoF stable-pose models: 6-DoFusion, StructDiffusion. (iii) Language-conditioned arrangement and free-space pointing: StructFormer, StructDiffusion, RoboPoint. Only group (i) maps naturally onto per-face predicates such as isSupportable(obj, face). None of these models outputs a pairwise "face A of obj2 supports obj1 on face B" judgement directly, so isP2PSupportive still needs geometry, rules, or a VLM on top.

### Cited Findings
- **Jiang, Lim, Zheng, Saxena, "Learning to Place New Objects in a Scene", IJRR 31(9), 2012.** Companion paper: "Learning to Place New Objects", ICRA 2012 (Jiang, Zheng, Lim, Saxena), first shown at an RSS 2011 workshop. Input: point clouds of the object and the scene. Output: scored placements. Method: a supervised graphical model with stability and object/area-relationship features; inference is an ILP solved by LP relaxation. Dataset: 98 objects in 16 categories, 40 placing areas. Robot results: 98% success for known objects and 82% for new objects placed stably. Applications: dish racks, bookshelf, fridge. Code: none found. Verdict: the historical anchor for "learned stable placement from point clouds", but too old and too heavily engineered to reuse; cite it for context. — [Stanford PDF](https://ai.stanford.edu/~asaxena/papers/learning_to_place_new_objects.pdf); [arXiv 1202.1694](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1202.1694)
- Follow-up: Jiang & Saxena, "Hallucinating Humans for Learning Robotic Placement of Objects" (2013 book chapter). It adds human-usage preferences to placement. — [Springer](https://www.springerprofessional.de/en/hallucinating-humans-for-learning-robotic-placement-of-objects/1989518)
- **UOP-Net: "Learning to Place Unseen Objects Stably using a Large-scale Simulation" (GIST; arXiv 2303.08387, 2023).** Published in IEEE RA-L 2024 [UNVERIFIED venue]. Input: a partial single-view point cloud of the object. Output: a segmentation of the single most stable plane. Training data: the UOP-Sim simulation dataset. On partial observations it beats a convex-hull stability baseline (CHSA). With the full shape visible, the convex-hull method does better. It can find implicit stable planes, such as a chair's four legs. Code, dataset and robot demos: gistailab.github.io/uop. Limits noted by later work: it assumes planar surfaces, outputs only one plane with no placeability score, ignores edge placements, and excludes spherical and small objects. Verdict: **the closest learned analogue to isSupportable(obj, face)**. With full or CAD geometry, though, the paper's own result says the convex-hull method is as good or better. — [arXiv 2303.08387](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.08387); [catalyzex summary](https://www.catalyzex.com/paper/learning-to-place-unseen-objects-stably-using); limitations per [arXiv 2510.14584](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14584v1)
- **"A Generalized Placeability Metric for …" (arXiv 2510.14584, 2025).** Positioned as overcoming UOP-Net's single-plane, planar-only limits with a general placeability score. Venue and code: [UNVERIFIED]. Verdict: worth reading as a scored per-region placeability signal, which could rank faces. — [arXiv 2510.14584](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14584v1)
- **6-DoFusion: "6-DoF Stability Field via Diffusion Models" (Yoneda, Jiang, Shakhnarovich, Walter; arXiv 2310.17649, 2023).** Venue: ICRA 2024 [UNVERIFIED]. Input: the scene plus the object. Output: SE(3) poses sampled from a learned, context-dependent distribution over stable poses, refined from a random initialization. Evaluated on placement and stacking, including novel classes; can also refine 3D pose estimators. Code: [UNVERIFIED]. Verdict: a strong generator of stable object-on-object poses, useful for motion-level grounding. It is not a symbolic classifier, but sampling-and-checking could approximate isP2PSupportive. — [arXiv 2310.17649](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.17649); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.17649)
- **StructFormer (Liu, Paxton, Hermans, Fox; ICRA 2022; arXiv 2110.10189).** Input: a partial-view scene point cloud plus a structured language command. Output: which objects to move and their target poses for multi-object structures (circles, table settings) with unseen objects. Code: a GitHub repo exists per ecosyste.ms. Verdict: semantic arrangement, not support or stability reasoning; low relevance. — [NVIDIA](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/srl/publication/liu-2022-struct-former); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.10189v1); [ecosyste.ms](https://science.ecosyste.ms/projects/51248)
- **StructDiffusion (Liu, Du, Hermans, Chernova, Paxton; RSS 2023; arXiv 2211.04604).** An earlier version had a different title. Input: a partial-view point cloud plus a language goal. Output: object poses from a diffusion model combined with an object-centric transformer, plus a learned collision discriminator. Building physically valid structures with unseen objects improves by about 16% on average over a multimodal-transformer baseline. Project page: structdiffusion.github.io (code [UNVERIFIED]). Verdict: covers "physically valid" stacking and structures, but as continuous pose generation; low to medium relevance. — [RSS proceedings](https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss19/p031.html); [NVIDIA](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2023-07_structdiffusion-language-guided-creation-physically-valid-structures-using)
- **RoboPoint (Yuan et al.; CoRL 2024, PMLR v270).** An open VLM instruction-tuned on synthetic data to point at image keypoints for spatial affordances such as free space and placement regions; the points are lifted to 3D with depth. Reported results: +21.8% spatial-affordance accuracy and +30.5% downstream success over GPT-4o and PIVOT. Code: robo-point.github.io. The "Where2Place" benchmark is commonly cited as introduced in RoboPoint, but this session could not confirm that [UNVERIFIED]. Verdict: answers "where on a surface" (pixel regions), not "can face X support object Y"; medium-low relevance. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v270/yuan25c.html); [arXiv 2406.10721](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.10721v1)
- Where2Fit, a free-space-on-table benchmark (464 samples: 200 generation and 264 judgment tasks), is described in the OmniEVA paper (arXiv 2509.09332). — [OmniEVA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.09332)
- Haustein et al., "Object Placement Planning and Optimization for Robot Manipulators" (arXiv 1907.02555; IROS 2019 [UNVERIFIED venue]). A planning and optimization treatment of placement with stability. — [arXiv 1907.02555](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.02555)
- Paxton et al., "Predicting Stable Configurations for Semantic Placement of Novel Objects" (CoRL 2021): learns pairwise relations and stability for placement from point clouds. **[UNVERIFIED: not returned by any search in this session; from background knowledge.]**

### Inferences
- For a 5-object CoppeliaSim scene with known meshes, learned placement predictors add little over analytic convex-hull/stable-pose computation. UOP-Net's own comparison shows the convex-hull baseline wins when the full shape is available. Learned predictors pay off with partial, real point clouds.
- None of the surveyed learned models outputs a pairwise, face-to-face support label. isP2PSupportive will need a composition step: candidate faces plus a geometric check (support-polygon containment, size ratio, flatness or concavity) or a VLM judgement.

### Gaps
- Exact code availability for 6-DoFusion, StructDiffusion and the 2510.14584 placeability metric could not be confirmed; the GitHub pages could not be fetched.
- No search found a "StablePose"-for-placement paper. "StablePose" (Shi et al., CVPR 2021) is a 6D *pose estimation* method built on geometrically stable patches. It is unrelated to placement despite the name. — [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/cvpr/2021/shi2021cvpr-stablepose)

## (A2) Stacking / physical stability prediction and geometric methods (plane fitting, support polygon, COM, stable resting poses)

### Takeaway
Image-based stability classifiers (ShapeStacks, Li et al. 2016, Lerer et al. 2016) learn binary "will this stack stand" labels on block worlds. They do not transfer well to kitchen objects and are rarely used in TAMP. For per-face support predicates, the dependable, standard tool is the analytic one: convex hull plus center of mass. A hull face is a stable resting face if the COM projects inside it, and each face gets a resting probability. Trimesh exposes this as `compute_stable_poses`, though this session could not open its documentation.

### Cited Findings
- **ShapeStacks (Groth, Fuchs, Posner, Vedaldi; ECCV 2018, pp. 702–717; arXiv 1804.08018).** About 20,000 simulated stacks of 2–6 objects (mostly 3 high), rendered from 16 views at 224×224. Labels: binary stable/unstable plus segmentation of the stability-violating parts. Split roughly 70/15/15. Dataset and code are public on the project page [UNVERIFIED link]. Verdict: a benchmark for image-based stability intuition on primitive shapes; low direct relevance to the kitchen objects. — [arXiv 1804.08018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.08018)
- **Li, Leonardis, Fritz, "Visual Stability Prediction and Its Application to Manipulation"** (arXiv 1609.04861; AAAI Spring Symposium 2017 [UNVERIFIED venue]). CNN stability prediction from images, applied to manipulation. Lerer et al. 2016 (PhysNet, block towers) and Jia et al. 2015 (3D reasoning about block stability) are the classic precursors. — [arXiv 1609.04861](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.04861); background per [ShapeStacks search summary](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.08018)
- **"Beyond Pick-and-Place: Tackling Robotic Stacking of Diverse Shapes"** (Lee et al., DeepMind; arXiv 2110.06192; CoRL 2021 [UNVERIFIED venue]). RL stacking of diverse, non-cuboid shapes. — [arXiv 2110.06192](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.06192)
- **StackGen: "Generating Stable Structures from Silhouettes via Diffusion"** (arXiv 2409.18098). Diffusion that generates stable block structures. — [arXiv 2409.18098](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.18098)
- **StableLego: "Stability Analysis of Block Stacking Assembly"** (arXiv 2402.10711). Force-based stability analysis for LEGO-style assemblies. — [alphaxiv listing](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2402.10711)
- **Convex-hull stable-face rule.** A hull face supports a stable placement on a horizontal surface if the COM, projected along the face normal, falls inside that face. One heuristic sets the resting probability of each face proportional to its projected solid-angle area on a sphere around the COM: larger faces mean more likely rests. — [US patent 11701777 (adaptive grasp planning for bin picking)](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11701777)
- The more rigorous version treats resting probability as the area of the face's basin (ascending manifold) on the Gauss sphere, under a quasi-static rolling model (Baktash, Sharp, Crane, Jacobson et al.). — [LIRIS slide deck](https://projet.liris.cnrs.fr/origami/topics/gdl/gdl_talk_68/LesDes.pdf); also "Let Them Fall Where They May" (Kriegman, capture regions of curved objects and polyhedra) — [UCSD](https://vision.ucsd.edu/node/225)
- **FlipToSee: "A Probabilistic Stable Placement Prior for Active Visual Exploration via Regrasping"** (arXiv 2609.20078, 2026). Uses a probabilistic stable-placement prior, appeared in the stable-poses search. Details [UNVERIFIED]. — [arXiv 2609.20078](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20078)
- **Trimesh `compute_stable_poses(mesh, center_mass, sigma, n_samples, threshold)`** returns stable-pose transforms and their probabilities, computed by perturbing the COM, dropping the object onto its convex hull faces, and counting which face it settles on. **[UNVERIFIED: trimesh.org was unreachable; signature and behavior are from background knowledge.]**

### Inferences
- For UTAMP-2's isSupportable(obj, face) with bounding-box faces, the cheapest check is: (1) compute hull stable faces (trimesh or COM-in-face), (2) map each stable hull face to the nearest axis-aligned bounding-box face by normal, and (3) mark that face supportable if its resting probability exceeds a threshold.
- For isSupportive(obj, face), check: (1) the face is roughly planar (RANSAC plane fit, residual below ε; reject concave or open faces such as the mug opening or the pan interior unless a rule allows "container" support), (2) its normal points up in the candidate pose, and (3) its usable area exceeds a minimum. For isP2PSupportive, add: the supported object's resting-face footprint (support polygon) fits inside the supporting face polygon, and the combined COM stays inside the lower object's support polygon.
- Image-only stability classifiers (ShapeStacks style) are a poor fit. They label whole stacks, not faces, and were trained on primitives.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm the exact trimesh algorithm, defaults, or how probabilities are computed (documentation unreachable).
- Found no published work that predicts "which face of object A can support object B" as a learned pairwise classifier; this appears to be an open niche. The absence is not verified exhaustively.

## (B1) U-TAMP / UTAMP-2 and Agostini's object-centred predicates

### Takeaway
U-TAMP (Agostini & Piater) folds motion constraints into task-level heuristic search through object-centric abstractions. UTAMP-2 (Niaz, Piater, Agostini 2026) adds VLM-derived affordance predicates (grasp, support, lift, slide) to a PDDL domain solved by Fast Downward. The figures in the brief (0.98 P/R/F1; 90% vs 100% success) could **not** be confirmed in this session: the paper PDF was unreachable and the snippets did not include them.

### Cited Findings
- **U-TAMP: "Unified Task and Motion Planning using Object-centric Abstractions of Motion Constraints"** (Agostini & Piater, University of Innsbruck; arXiv 2312.17605; v1 29 Dec 2023, v2 30 Jan 2026). Folds TAMP into a single heuristic search using object-centric abstractions of motion constraints (grasping, placement, kinematics). Plans translate directly into object and motion parameters for execution. Includes perception and execution mechanisms linking sensing to task planning. Venue: [UNVERIFIED]. Code: none found. — [arXiv 2312.17605](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17605v1); [arXiv html v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.17605v2)
- **UTAMP-2: "Leveraging Inter-object Affordances for Efficient Planning in Contact-rich Tasks"** (Pouya P. Niaz, Justus Piater, Alejandro Agostini; arXiv 2608.25641, submitted 26 Aug 2026). Builds on U-TAMP. A VLM generates affordance abstractions for grasp and support constraints. The PDDL domain handles grasping, supporting, lifting and sliding at task level, so motion planning need not reason about them. Pipeline: perception → affordance detection → symbolic mapping → Fast Downward → RRT-Connect. Attributes come either from ground truth or from a VLM and are converted to predicates such as canLift and isSupportive. Reported results: higher planning success, and planning times one to two orders of magnitude faster than original U-TAMP and a VLM-based planner, on simulated kitchen-table organization. Limitations: "simplifying assumptions and rules in affordance assignment and motion planning". — [arXiv 2608.25641](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25641); [alphaxiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.25641)
- Niaz's KIT H2T profile lists an affiliation with the University of Innsbruck. — [KIT](https://h2t.iar.kit.edu/pouya-pourakbarian-niaz.php)
- **Agostini, Saveriano, Lee, Piater, "Manipulation Planning Using Object-Centered Predicates and Hierarchical Decomposition of Contextual Actions"**, IEEE RA-L 5(4):5629–5636, 2020, DOI 10.1109/LRA.2020.3009063. Task planning uses object-centred geometric relations that consistently describe changes in object configuration space. Execution uses a symbol-to-motion hierarchical decomposition that depends on consecutive plan actions. — [TUM portal](https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/manipulation-planning-using-object-centered-predicates-and-hierar-2/); related preprint "Efficient State Abstraction using Object-centered Predicates for Manipulation Planning" (Agostini & Lee, arXiv 2007.08251) describes universal pick/place operators with geometric and force consistency. Whether the preprint and the RA-L paper are the same work is [UNVERIFIED]. — [arXiv 2007.08251](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.08251)

### Inferences
- UTAMP-2 performs well with a VLM for attributes plus a rule parser on only 5 objects. This suggests support-predicate errors come mainly from attribute misjudgments (e.g., flatness or rigidity of the mug or pan faces), not from parsing. The remaining 10% planning gap (if the brief's numbers hold) likely traces to a few wrong pairwise support facts that prune the only valid stacking order or permit an unstable one.

### Gaps
- Not verified from a primary source: the use of o4-mini, the predicate names isSupportable and isP2PSupportive, the 0.98 precision/recall/F1, and 90% (VLM) vs 100% (ground truth) planning success. The snippets mentioned only canLift and isSupportive.
- Code release for U-TAMP and UTAMP-2: none found.

## (B2) Perception → symbolic predicates for TAMP/PDDL (learned classifiers, predicate invention, VLM grounders, distillation)

### Takeaway
There are three families. (1) Learned predicate classifiers trained from action labels or demonstrations (Migimatsu & Bohg 2022; predicate invention by Silver et al. 2023, and IVNTR). (2) VLM-queried neuro-symbolic predicates (VisualPredicator, ICLR 2025; S3E). (3) VLM/LLM pipelines that write the PDDL problem or scene from images (ViLaIn, ICRA 2024; ViLaIn-TAMP 2025; AutoGPT+P, RSS 2024). Explicit teacher-VLM → student-classifier distillation for predicates was not found as a published method.

### Cited Findings
- **Migimatsu & Bohg, "Grounding Predicates through Actions"** (ICRA 2022, pp. 3498–3504; arXiv 2109.14718). Input: RGB video of human activity. Labels are derived automatically from PDDL action pre- and post-conditions, so annotators tag only actions. Output: per-predicate relational classifiers, used for closed-loop task planning on a real robot. Verdict: the most relevant template for training a small predicate classifier cheaply. Labels for support predicates could likewise come from successful or failed place actions in CoppeliaSim. Code: [UNVERIFIED]. — [arXiv 2109.14718](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.14718); [Stanford ICRA 2022 blog](https://ai.stanford.edu/blog/icra-2022)
- **Silver, Chitnis et al., "Predicate Invention for Bilevel Planning"** (AAAI 2023, vol. 37, pp. 12120–12129). Learns predicates from demonstrations by hill-climbing over a grammar of predicates, using a surrogate for planning efficiency. Outperforms six baselines across four environments. Builds on "Learning Neuro-Symbolic Skills for Bilevel Planning" (Silver et al., CoRL 2022) and "Learning Symbolic Operators for TAMP" (Silver et al., IROS 2021) **[UNVERIFIED: those two venues are from background knowledge]**. Code: the `predicators` repo by Learning-and-Intelligent-Systems [UNVERIFIED]. — [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/26429); [MIT-IBM blog](https://mitibmwatsonailab.mit.edu/research/blog/predicate-invention-for-bilevel-planning)
- **IVNTR**, a follow-up claimed to be the first bilevel planner that learns *neural* predicates directly from demonstrations, alternating symbolic effect learning with neural function learning. Venue and year: [UNVERIFIED]. — per [Silver search summary](https://mitibmwatsonailab.mit.edu/research/blog/predicate-invention-for-bilevel-planning)
- **VisualPredicator (Liang, Kumar, …, Silver, Tenenbaum, Ellis et al.; ICLR 2025; arXiv 2410.23156).** Neuro-Symbolic Predicates are Python snippets that can call VLMs for perceptual properties. An online algorithm invents predicates and learns abstract world models. It beats hierarchical RL, VLM planning and symbolic predicate invention across five simulated domains on sample complexity, out-of-distribution generalization and interpretability. Stated limitation: models built on the raw set of proposed predicates are not robust, because of VLM evaluation noise, so predicates are subselected. VLM evaluation accuracy is a primary limitation. — [arXiv 2410.23156](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.23156); [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/29691)
- **S3E (Semantic Symbolic State Estimation)** (ICRA 2025 workshop paper). Uses a VLM directly as a multi-label symbolic-state classifier, with over 90% state-estimation precision in simulated and real robot experiments. — [Mines ICRA-WS PDF](https://dyalab.mines.edu/2025/icra-workshop/5.pdf)
- **ViLaIn (Shirai et al.; ICRA 2024; arXiv 2311.00967).** LLM plus VLM (object detector) generate PDDL problems from language and scene images, and refine them using planner error feedback. Results: over 99% syntactically correct problems, over 58% valid plans. Introduces the ProDG dataset. Code: github.com/omron-sinicx/vilain. — [arXiv 2311.00967](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.00967); [GitHub](https://github.com/omron-sinicx/vilain)
- **ViLaIn-TAMP (Siburian, Shirai et al.; arXiv 2506.03270, 2025).** Combines a vision-language interpreter, a sequence-before-satisfy TAMP module, and corrective planning from grounded failure feedback. Mean success is more than 30% higher than without corrective planning (bimanual, long-horizon). — [arXiv 2506.03270](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.03270)
- **AutoGPT+P (Birr, Pohl, Younes, Asfour, KIT; RSS 2024; arXiv 2402.10778).** Object detection plus an LLM (ChatGPT)-generated object–affordance mapping yields an affordance scene representation for an LLM-driven planner with exploration, alternatives and partial plans. Results: 98% on SayCan instructions (vs 81% reported for SayCan) and 79% on 150 missing-object scenarios. Code and dataset public. Verdict: closest in spirit to UTAMP-2's attribute → affordance mapping, but its affordances are object-level (e.g., "cut-with"), not face-level support. — [RSS proceedings](https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss20/p112.html); [arXiv 2402.10778](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.10778)
- **"Planning with affordances: Integrating learned affordance models and symbolic planning"** (arXiv 2502.02768, 2025). Learned affordance models feeding symbolic planning. Details [UNVERIFIED]. — [arXiv 2502.02768](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02768v1)
- **"Guiding Long-Horizon Task and Motion Planning with Vision Language Models"** (arXiv 2410.02193) and **UniPlan** (arXiv 2602.08537, a unified PDDL formulation for VLM mobile-manipulation planning). — [arXiv 2410.02193](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.02193); [arXiv 2602.08537](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.08537)
- **Symbolic Manipulation Planning with Discovered Object and Relational Predicates** (arXiv 2401.01123). Predicate discovery from interaction. — [arXiv 2401.01123](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.01123)
- **Distillation:** the search found no paper that explicitly distils VLM predicate judgements into a small student classifier for planning. The nearest neighbours are S3E (a VLM used directly as a classifier), Migimatsu & Bohg (small classifiers from action-derived labels), and SelfReVision (small-VLM self-improvement for plan generation, not predicates). — [SelfReVision](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2507.08224)
- "pix2predicate" and "Generalized Planning with VLM predicates": no search in this session confirmed these titles.

### Inferences
- A practical recipe for our module, combining Migimatsu & Bohg with VisualPredicator: use o4-mini plus rules, or geometric checks, as a teacher to label (object, face) and (face, object, face, object) tuples in CoppeliaSim. Validate the labels with physics roll-outs (place, settle, check displacement). Then train a small point-cloud or crop classifier. Physics-validated labels avoid copying VLM noise.

### Gaps
- Could not verify code for VisualPredicator (a GitHub release is likely but unconfirmed), Migimatsu & Bohg, or the predicators repo.
- No published teacher-VLM → student predicate-classifier distillation for TAMP found. This may be a genuine gap or a search limitation.

## (B3) Evidence on how predicate errors affect planning success

### Takeaway
Across benchmarks, wrong grounded predicates are the dominant failure source in VLM-as-grounder pipelines. False negatives create dead ends: a needed precondition is falsely unsatisfied. False positives create invalid or looping actions. How much each benchmark recovers depends on replanning and closed-loop feedback. Grounding helps where images are fully observable (Blocksworld) and hurts under partial observability (Household).

### Cited Findings
- **ViPlan (arXiv 2505.13180, 2025).** An open benchmark comparing VLM-as-grounder (VLM evaluates PDDL predicates; a symbolic planner plans) with VLM-as-planner. Blocksworld: grounder 46% vs direct planning 9%. Household: direct VLM planners 34% vs grounders 5%, which the authors attribute to partial observability. Setup: 2 domains × 3 difficulty splits × 25 tasks; 21 open VLMs screened. Chain-of-thought gave no significant benefit. Failure modes: a falsely "unsatisfied" precondition leads to a dead end, and a falsely "satisfied" one leads to repeated invalid actions. — [arXiv 2505.13180](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13180v2)
- **UniPlan (arXiv 2602.08537).** The largest failure category was PDDL grounding errors: objects identified but their states wrong, making problems unsolvable. The authors call for more robust visual state estimation. — [arXiv 2602.08537](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.08537)
- **VisualPredicator.** Noisy predicates are sometimes dropped during predicate selection, allowing recovery, and sometimes not. VLM evaluation accuracy is named a primary limitation. — [arXiv 2410.23156](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.23156)
- **Azran et al., "VLM-as-probabilistic-grounder" (AAMAS 2026).** Treats VLM predicate outputs as a distribution over symbolic states and plans with a conformant planner over a few likely states. Reports better robustness and task success than deterministic grounding in simulated household tasks; the approximation can make planning conservative. A related arXiv 2609.16884 (Sept 2026) is [UNVERIFIED]. — [Telecom Paris NEXUS PDF](https://nexus.telecom-paris.fr/papers/26NEXUS-Azran.pdf)
- **SYMBOLIZER (arXiv 2604.17830, 2026).** Argues that systematic search can partly absorb initial-state grounding errors if the representation stays tractable and keeps goal-relevant structure. — [arXiv 2604.17830](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.17830)
- **ViLaIn-TAMP.** Corrective re-planning from grounded failure feedback adds more than 30% mean success, so closed-loop correction recovers many grounding errors. — [arXiv 2506.03270](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.03270)
- **ViLaIn.** Syntactic validity is over 99% but valid plans only over 58%, a large gap between "well-formed" and "correct" problem files. — [arXiv 2311.00967](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.00967)

### Inferences
- In a 5-object stacking problem, the error asymmetry matters. A false-negative isP2PSupportive may remove the only feasible stacking order, making the problem unsolvable. A false-positive may produce a plan that fails physically at execution. This is consistent with the brief's figures (0.98 F1 yet 90% vs 100% success): plan success is roughly the probability that *every* relevant predicate is correct, so even 2% predicate error compounds across the roughly 5×6 face and roughly 5²×6² pairwise tuples. This compounding argument is an inference, not a cited result.
- Recommended mitigations from the literature: probabilistic or conformant grounding (Azran), planner-feedback correction (ViLaIn, ViLaIn-TAMP), and geometric or physics verification of the VLM output.

### Gaps
- Found no study that quantitatively sweeps predicate error rate against planning success in a controlled way (e.g., noise injection) for manipulation PDDL. This would be a valuable ablation for our module.
- The UTAMP-2 error breakdown (which predicates the VLM got wrong) is not available without the full paper.
