# Ground UTAMP-2 predicates with geometry, then learn

No published model outputs UTAMP-2's predicates directly. No grasp detector emits canGraspFrom(obj, member, palm, finger1, finger2), no placement model emits a pairwise face-to-face isP2PSupportive, and no affordance dataset uses "support" in UTAMP-2's sense. The fastest credible front end is therefore deterministic geometry over the member boxes already authored in the supervisors' CoppeliaSim scene, with off-the-shelf models as candidate generators and priors. Those models are a learned grasp detector (GraspGen or Contact-GraspNet) whose 6-DoF grasps are binned onto box faces, CoppeliaSim's colour-coded instance masks and metric depth for perception, and a category/material mass prior with a torque check for canLift. This matters because UTAMP-2's own results locate the weak point exactly here. Ground-truth predicates give **100% plan success**, while o4-mini-detected predicates give **90%** even at a **mean affordance precision/recall/F1 of 0.98**, and the failures come from inconsistent VLM attribute detection that drops support affordances ([UTAMP-2, arXiv 2608.25641](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25641)). For the two-to-three-week demo, the recommendation is a geometric predicate generator checked against the paper's ground truth, with GraspGen as the learned grasp source. For a publishable contribution, the strongest option is to extend Maksym's VLM-to-DINOv2 distillation to face-level, physics-validated support and grasp predicates, paired with a controlled study of how predicate errors become planning failures. The literature search found no published work occupying any of these three niches: pairwise face-support classification, VLM-teacher distillation of TAMP predicates, and predicate-noise-versus-plan-success sweeps.

## The scene already solves members, so perception must solve faces

UTAMP-2 (Niaz, Piater, Agostini, arXiv 2608.25641, 26 Aug 2026) defines its predicates over **members**: rigidly attached sub-objects such as body, handle and edge, each with a bounding box parallel to the parent's and defined by the authors per object. It also defines **parts**: the six box sides plus "inside". The Table I predicates that the front end must produce are isSupportive ?obj ?part, isSupportable ?obj ?part, isP2PSupportive ?part2 ?obj2 ?part1 ?obj1, canGraspFrom ?obj1 ?o1-mem ?o1m-hp ?o1m-hf1 ?o1m-hf2 (palm, finger 1 and finger 2 contact parts) and canLift ?obj1 ?o1-mem. Alongside these sit canSlide, isMember, isOpposite, isAdjacent, base, oc and force. Grasps come in three types (wrap, edge, handle), and the actions are pick-support, place-support and slide-support. The benchmark has **127 valid stack configurations of 2 to 5 objects** with the goal of stacking everything on the tray, solved with Fast Downward and RRT-Connect via SimOMPL in CoppeliaSim. UTAMP-2-GT reaches 100% success, UTAMP-2-VLM 90%, other methods below 50%, and a pure VLM planner 39%. Planning takes **0.17 ± 0.05 s (GT) and 0.20 ± 0.08 s (UTAMP-2-VLM) against 66.9 ± 25.2 s** for the VLM planner, and sliding-task success is 1.0, 0.81 and 0.41 respectively. The PDDL domain and problem files are on an OSF link given in the paper, and no public code release was found ([UTAMP-2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25641)). The predecessor U-TAMP folds motion constraints into task-level heuristic search through object-centric abstractions ([U-TAMP, arXiv 2312.17605](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17605v1)). Agostini's earlier RA-L 2020 paper established the object-centred predicate style ([Agostini et al., RA-L 2020](https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/manipulation-planning-using-object-centered-predicates-and-hierar-2/)).

The supervisors' scene file (panda_orgn_kitchenware_ompl_v006-2.ttt) changes the shape of the problem. It already contains named member shapes for every object: cutting_board_body/handle; frying_pan_body/handle/edge_front/edge_left/edge_right; glass_mug_body/handle/edge_front/edge_left/edge_right; salt_shaker_body; and wooden_tray_body/handle_front/handle_back/edge_left/edge_right. It also contains grasp_*, place_* and support_* frames, a visionSensor, camera1 and a Gripper_Camera (supervisor-supplied scene file, decoded). In simulation, member detection is therefore *already answered by authoring*. The unsolved perception step is deciding which of the six faces plus "inside" of each member supports, rests on, or is contacted by the palm and fingers, and how heavy each object is. Part detection becomes valuable only as a way to remove the hand-authoring step, and that becomes a contribution in itself, because the authored members provide a free evaluation ground truth.

The table below maps each predicate to the evidence it needs and the source this report recommends. The rest of the report justifies each row.

| Predicate | What it actually depends on | Recommended demo source | Learned alternative (later) |
|---|---|---|---|
| isSupportive(obj, part) | Part is upward-facing, flat or a floored cavity ("inside" of tray/pan), large enough | Geometric rule on member box + mesh | Face-level student classifier |
| isSupportable(obj, part) | Object can rest stably on that part (COM projects inside contact polygon) | Convex-hull stable-face test | UOP-Net-style stable-plane prediction |
| isP2PSupportive(p2, o2, p1, o1) | Footprint of o1/p1 fits inside p2's support region; combined COM stays inside | Geometric composition of the two above | Pairwise student (open niche) |
| canGraspFrom(obj, mem, palm, f1, f2) | Member width along closing axis ≤ gripper opening; approach collision-free | Face-pair width test + GraspGen/Contact-GraspNet grasps binned to faces | Same, with learned scores |
| canLift(obj, mem) | Mass and lever arm about the grasped member versus payload/torque | CoppeliaSim mass or category prior, with interval and torque check | VLM/material → density × volume |

## Grasp detectors produce candidates, and face binning turns them into canGraspFrom

Every modern 6-DoF parallel-jaw detector outputs continuous gripper poses, not symbolic grasps, so any of them needs a binning step. A detector is a good fit when it emits a full SE(3) frame plus an opening width or contact point, runs on CoppeliaSim's single-view depth, and models the Panda hand. Two candidates meet the Panda requirement on primary evidence. **GraspGen** (NVIDIA, arXiv 2507.13097, accepted to ICRA 2026) ships Franka Panda, Robotiq-2F-140 and suction models. It takes a segmented object point cloud or mesh and returns top-k 6-DoF poses with discriminator scores at about 20 Hz. It was trained on **over 57M grasps on 8,515 Objaverse-XL (LVIS) objects** with the dataset under CC BY 4.0, and it reports **81.3% real-robot success, +17.6 points over AnyGrasp and +28 over M2T2**. Its code licence is NVIDIA's, with commercial use routed to NVIDIA licensing. It requires a PointNet++ CUDA build, and its PTv3 backbone does not run on CUDA 12.8/Blackwell GPUs, so the lab GPU should be checked first ([GraspGen GitHub](https://github.com/NVlabs/GraspGen); [arXiv 2507.13097](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.13097)). **VGN** (CoRL 2020) used a real Panda with a wrist RealSense, plans in about 10 ms, clears 92% of objects in real clutter, and is BSD-3-Clause ([arXiv 2101.01132](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.01132); [ethz-asl/vgn](https://github.com/ethz-asl/vgn)). VGN, however, works in a **30 cm TSDF cube**, which the frying pan with its handle and the wooden tray probably exceed.

**Contact-GraspNet** (ICRA 2021) has the most face-friendly output. Each grasp is anchored at an observed contact point and carries a predicted width, so the second finger contact is roughly contact + width × closing axis. Its input is a depth map plus camera intrinsics K and an optional segmentation map, which matches a CoppeliaSim vision sensor exactly. It was trained on about 17M ACRONYM grasps over ShapeNet meshes and reports over 90% real success in structured clutter ([NVlabs/contact_graspnet](https://github.com/NVlabs/contact_graspnet); [arXiv 2103.14127](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.14127)). Its drawbacks are practical. It pins TensorFlow 2.2 and CUDA 11.1 and needs at least 8 GB of VRAM. The only PyTorch port is unofficial and "may not match" the paper ([elchun/contact_graspnet_pytorch](https://github.com/elchun/contact_graspnet_pytorch)). Its licence is reported by a third party as non-commercial NVIDIA ([NGDF README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/NGDF/)). That the ACRONYM gripper is the Panda is consistent with NVIDIA lineage, and GraspGen describes building "a similar dataset for the Franka Panda gripper", but no primary page confirmed it ([NVlabs/acronym](https://github.com/NVlabs/acronym)) [unverified].

The remaining options fit worse. **AnyGrasp** (T-RO 2023) is a closed binary SDK licensed per machine feature ID, with replies in about five workdays, which complicates containers and clusters. It returns graspnetAPI GraspGroups (rotation, translation, width, depth, score) for a generic parallel-jaw box gripper ([anygrasp_sdk](https://github.com/graspnet/anygrasp_sdk); [graspnetAPI docs](https://graspnetapi.readthedocs.io/en/latest/graspnetAPI.html)). Its open sibling, graspnet-baseline, is non-commercial ([graspnet-baseline fork](https://github.com/xiaomi2077/graspnet-baseline)). **M2T2** (CoRL 2023) predicts both grasps and orientation-aware placements from a scene cloud, but its training data is unreleased and its licence unknown ([NVlabs/M2T2](https://github.com/NVlabs/M2T2)). **GG-CNN** (RSS 2018) and **GR-ConvNet** (IROS 2020, 97.7% on Cornell, about 20 ms) are planar 4-DoF detectors, so they can only express palm = top ([arXiv 1804.05172](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.05172v2); [arXiv 1909.04810](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.04810)). That rules out the handle and edge grasps the pan, board and tray require. GIGA and Edge Grasp Network are MIT-licensed VGN descendants that inherit its workspace constraints ([GIGA](https://github.com/UT-Austin-RPL/GIGA); [Edge Grasp](https://github.com/HaojHuang/Edge-Grasp-Network)).

The binning step below is our own design; no source implements it. First, transform each grasp into the parent box frame, using CoppeliaSim's ground-truth pose or an OBB of the segmented cloud. Assign the member whose sub-box contains the midpoint of the two contacts. Set palm = the face whose outward normal is most anti-parallel to the approach axis. Set f1, f2 = the faces hit by rays along ± the closing axis, or the predicted contacts directly for Contact-GraspNet. Reject the grasp if the member's extent along the closing axis exceeds the Panda's 80 mm opening (Franka spec; not re-verified in these notes). Finally, aggregate scores per (member, palm, {f1, f2}) tuple and threshold. The literature closest to this is **AGFP-Net**, which predicts feasibility over a discrete set of grasp types as a heuristic for a symbolic planner ([IEEE 10341257](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/10341257/)) [authors and venue unverified], and **PS-CNN**, which maps primitive shape classes to parametrised grasp families and reports 94.2% real success ([arXiv 1909.08508](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08508)). Neither works at the level of palm and finger faces, so the binning step is new relative to both.

## Support predicates are geometry problems no learned model solves pairwise

The learned placement literature splits three ways, and only one group maps onto per-face predicates. Per-object stable-surface predictors are the closest analogue to isSupportable. Jiang et al. scored placements from point clouds with 98% success for known objects and 82% for new ones ([IJRR 2012 PDF](https://ai.stanford.edu/~asaxena/papers/learning_to_place_new_objects.pdf)). UOP-Net segments the single most stable plane from a partial cloud and was trained on the UOP-Sim dataset ([arXiv 2303.08387](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.08387)) [RA-L venue unverified]. Its own comparison has a direct consequence for us: the convex-hull baseline beats it **when the full shape is visible**, which is precisely the CoppeliaSim case. Later work criticises it for assuming planar surfaces, returning only one plane and ignoring edges ([arXiv 2510.14584](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14584v1)). The second group, generative stable-pose models (6-DoFusion, arXiv 2310.17649; StructDiffusion, RSS 2023), samples continuous SE(3) placements ([arXiv 2310.17649](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.17649); [RSS 2023](https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss19/p031.html)). These suit motion-level grounding and could approximate isP2PSupportive by sample-and-check, but they emit no symbols. The third group, language-driven arrangement and pointing models (StructFormer, ICRA 2022; RoboPoint, CoRL 2024, +21.8% spatial-affordance accuracy over GPT-4o), answers "where" rather than "can this face hold that object" ([StructFormer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.10189v1); [RoboPoint](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v270/yuan25c.html)). Image-level stability classifiers such as ShapeStacks (ECCV 2018, about 20,000 primitive stacks) label whole stacks rather than faces ([arXiv 1804.08018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.08018)).

The dependable tool is analytic. A convex-hull face is a stable resting face if the centre of mass projects inside it, and the probability of resting on it scales with its basin area on the Gauss sphere ([US patent 11701777](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11701777); [LIRIS slides](https://projet.liris.cnrs.fr/origami/topics/gdl/gdl_talk_68/LesDes.pdf)). The trimesh library packages this as `compute_stable_poses`, but its documentation was unreachable during research, so the signature and probability semantics are [unverified] ([trimesh.org](https://trimesh.org)). For UTAMP-2 the rules compose naturally. isSupportable(o, p) holds when a stable hull face maps by normal onto box part p with resting probability above a threshold. isSupportive(o, p) holds when p is upward-facing and flat (RANSAC plane residual below ε) with enough area, *or* when p is "inside" and the cavity has a flat floor. That second clause is not a corner case: the benchmark goal is everything on the tray, so the tray's "inside" is the most important support part in the dataset, and the pan interior plays the same role. isP2PSupportive(p2, o2, p1, o1) then requires the footprint of o1 resting on p1 to fit inside the support region of p2, and the combined centre of mass to stay inside o2's own support polygon. These rules are our own synthesis. They need no training data and give a reproducible, inspectable baseline against which the o4-mini output can be diffed.

**Verdict:** adopt analytic geometry for all three support predicates in the demo. Cite UOP-Net as the learned per-object analogue and 6-DoFusion as a pose-level alternative. Treat pairwise face-to-face support classification as an open niche, since no learned pairwise support model was found, though that search was not exhaustive.

## Why 0.98 F1 still cost UTAMP-2 one plan in ten

The grounding literature explains the 90%-versus-100% gap and points to the fixes. ViPlan, which compares a VLM-as-grounder (the VLM evaluates PDDL predicates and a symbolic planner plans) against a VLM-as-planner, names the two failure modes precisely. A falsely *unsatisfied* precondition creates a dead end, and a falsely *satisfied* one produces repeated invalid actions. Grounders win in fully observable Blocksworld (46% vs 9%) and lose in partially observable Household (5% vs 34%) ([ViPlan, arXiv 2505.13180](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13180v2)). UTAMP-2's own diagnosis, inconsistent attribute detection producing *missing* support affordances, is the dead-end case ([UTAMP-2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25641)). UniPlan likewise found PDDL grounding errors to be its largest failure category ([arXiv 2602.08537](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.08537)). VisualPredicator (ICLR 2025) names VLM evaluation noise as a primary limitation and subselects predicates to survive it ([arXiv 2410.23156](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.23156)). ViLaIn reaches over 99% syntactically valid PDDL problems but only over 58% valid plans ([arXiv 2311.00967](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.00967)).

Our inference from these results is that a plan fails whenever *any* predicate on its critical path is wrong. A 5-object scene has 35 (object, part) pairs and therefore roughly 980 ordered pairwise isP2PSupportive tuples, so a few errors are enough to delete the only valid stacking order. Three published mitigations apply. The first is probabilistic grounding with a conformant planner over a few likely states, which improved robustness at the cost of conservatism ([Azran et al., AAMAS 2026](https://nexus.telecom-paris.fr/papers/26NEXUS-Azran.pdf)). The second is corrective replanning from grounded failure feedback, worth more than 30% mean success in ViLaIn-TAMP ([arXiv 2506.03270](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.03270)). The third is geometric or physics verification of VLM outputs, which is exactly what the analytic rules above provide. SYMBOLIZER argues that systematic search partly absorbs initial-state errors if the representation stays tractable ([arXiv 2604.17830](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.17830)). No paper the researchers found sweeps injected predicate-error rate against planning success in manipulation PDDL. UTAMP-2's 127-configuration benchmark is an ideal testbed for that sweep.

On learning predicates rather than prompting for them, Migimatsu and Bohg (ICRA 2022) is the closest template. They train per-predicate classifiers using labels derived automatically from PDDL pre- and post-conditions, so annotators tag only actions ([arXiv 2109.14718](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.14718)). Silver et al. invent predicates from demonstrations ([AAAI 2023](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/26429)). S3E uses a VLM directly as a multi-label symbolic-state classifier at over 90% precision ([S3E workshop paper](https://dyalab.mines.edu/2025/icra-workshop/5.pdf)). AutoGPT+P builds object-level affordance maps for an LLM planner at 98% on SayCan instructions, but its affordances are object-level rather than face-level ([RSS 2024](https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss20/p112.html)). Physically grounded VLMs (PG-InstructBLIP) raised LLM-planner accuracy from 56.9% to 88.2% by answering physical-concept queries ([arXiv 2309.02561](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.02561v4)). **No published work distils a teacher VLM's predicate judgements into a small student for TAMP**, and the closest neighbours are S3E and Migimatsu and Bohg ([SelfReVision search context](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2507.08224)). Maksym's thesis did exactly that for six inter-object predicates. A GPT-5-mini teacher was distilled into a DINOv2 + MLP pairwise student that runs in about 100 ms per scene against about 18 s for the live VLM, at 79.9% accuracy against human labels versus the teacher's 83.2% (thesis, internal). This gives the publishable track a running start.

## Members, mass and materials: author offline, look up priors, verify geometrically

**Part and affordance detectors give priors, not members.** The classic affordance datasets use "support" in a tool-use sense. In UMD (ICRA 2015; 7 affordances over 17 tool categories), "support" means "flat parts that can hold loose material (turners/spatulas)", and "wrap-grasp" means "can be held with the hand and palm (the outside of a cup)" ([UMD part-affordance dataset](https://users.umiacs.umd.edu/~fer/affordance/part-affordance-dataset/index.html)). IIT-AFF (IROS 2017; 9 affordances, 10 categories including cup and pan; 8,835 images) has the same orientation ([IIT-AFF](https://sites.google.com/site/iitaffdataset/)). Neither covers trays, cutting boards or salt shakers. AffordanceNet (ICRA 2018) is a Caffe-only, MIT-licensed two-branch Mask R-CNN at 150 ms per image with no official PyTorch port ([nqanh/affordance-net](https://github.com/nqanh/affordance-net)). 3D AffordanceNet (CVPR 2021; about 23k PartNet shapes, 18 labels including grasp, wrap-grasp, contain and support; MIT) learned "support" on furniture ([Gorilla-Lab-SCUT/AffordanceNet](https://github.com/Gorilla-Lab-SCUT/AffordanceNet)). OpenAD (IROS 2023) and LASO (CVPR 2024) add language queries over the same distribution ([arXiv 2303.02401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02401); [LASO](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Li_LASO_Language-guided_Affordance_Segmentation_on_3D_Object_CVPR_2024_paper.html)). Verdict: these are useful at most as a handle/body prior for the mug and pan. Skip them for support.

**Open-vocabulary part segmenters can replace hand-authored members.** On the 2D side, **VLPart** (ICCV 2023, MIT) detects objects and then their parts from text prompts, trained on PACO's 641K part masks ([facebookresearch/VLPart](https://github.com/facebookresearch/VLPart); [PACO, CVPR 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Ramanathan_PACO_Parts_and_Attributes_of_Common_Objects_CVPR_2023_paper.html)). Its object-then-parts structure mirrors UTAMP-2's parent box → member hierarchy, but PACO's coverage of mug, pan, tray and chopping board is [unverified]. The published reference pipeline is **AnyPart**: GroundingDINO, then open-vocabulary part segmentation, then a grasp predictor. It picks the correct part 88.57% of the time with **69.52% end-to-end grasp success** over 360 trials ([arXiv 2406.05951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05951v1)). On the 3D side, which suits CoppeliaSim's complete meshes, **PartField** (ICCV 2025, NVIDIA) predicts a part feature field in one feed-forward pass that clusters hierarchically ([PartField](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/partfield-release)) [licence unverified]. **Find3D** (ICCV 2025) takes a point cloud and a free-text part query such as "handle" ([Find3D](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Ma_Find_Any_Part_in_3D_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf)) [licence unverified]. SAMPart3D clusters scale-conditioned features and names the parts with a VLM ([arXiv 2411.07184](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.07184v1)). PartSLIP++ is MIT-licensed but needs few-shot tuning per category ([PartSLIP2](https://github.com/zyc00/PartSLIP2)). "Edge" members, such as tray rims and pan lips, are not a standard part class in any of these models, so they are better defined geometrically as thin slabs at the parent-box boundary (our inference). Verdict: run PartField or Find3D **offline on each mesh**, fit parent-aligned boxes, and score them against the supervisors' authored members. That turns a manual step into a measurable one.

**canLift should be a torque test on a mass interval, not a mass regression.** The edge-grasped frying pan fails on lever arm, not on total mass, and none of the mass estimators below output a centre of mass. In simulation the honest baseline is CoppeliaSim's ground-truth mass, or a category/material lookup with an interval. canLift(o, m) is then false if the upper mass bound exceeds payload minus gripper mass, or if mass × g × lever arm about member m exceeds the wrist torque limit. The 3 kg payload is a Franka spec not re-verified here. **image2mass** (CoRL 2017) predicts mass from an RGB image plus dimensions via density × volume, trained on about 150k Amazon products ([PMLR](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v78/standley17a/standley17a.pdf)). Follow-ups report its ALDE at 0.47 to 0.67, roughly a factor of 1.6 to 2 error ([arXiv 2601.20303](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20303); [arXiv 2305.14352](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14352)), and **12.5 kg ADE on ABO-500** ([PhysGS, arXiv 2511.18570](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18570)). That is too coarse near a 3 kg threshold, and the code release is [unverified]. **PG-InstructBLIP** (ICRA 2024; PhysObjects: 39.6K crowd-sourced and 417K automated labels; weights on Hugging Face) scores **fragility at 94.6% but mass at 80.0%**, and its mass answers are pairwise comparisons rather than kilograms ([arXiv 2309.02561](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.02561v4); [HF model](https://huggingface.co/bidiptas/PG-InstructBLIP)). That makes it a good source for "glass mug is fragile, so restrict what it supports". NeRF2Physics (CVPR 2024), PUGS (2025) and PhysGS (2025) reconstruct the object, ask an LLM for candidate materials and densities, and integrate to get mass ([arXiv 2404.04242](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.04242v1); [arXiv 2502.12231](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12231v2)). Their ABO-500 numbers conflict across papers, with SiPhy's re-run placing PUGS below NeRF2Physics ([SiPhy, arXiv 2607.22355](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.22355)), and all of them need multi-view capture. GPT-4V estimated 20 portable ABO objects at **0.39 kg MAE with 75% within ±30%** ([PhysGen, arXiv 2409.18964](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.18964)). That is enough for coarse classes, provided an interval is kept. For material, the main dense datasets are MINC (CVPR 2015, 23 classes) and DMS (ECCV 2022, 44,560 images, 3.2M segments), and both cover wood, glass and metal ([MINC](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2015/papers/Bell_Material_Recognition_in_2015_CVPR_paper.pdf); [DMS](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136680440.pdf)). Classifiers trained on real photos, however, are likely to transfer poorly to OpenGL renders. One 2026 assembly planner uses a payload-aware capability predicate, but it takes masses from design data rather than vision ([arXiv 2601.00978](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.00978)) [details unverified]. Vision-to-canLift with explicit uncertainty therefore remains unclaimed, though on its own it is a modest contribution.

**The glass mug is a sim-to-real hazard, not a sim problem.** A PhoXi depth baseline grasped transparent objects **0/10** where Dex-NeRF reached 10/10 ([arXiv 2110.14217](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.14217)). ClearGrasp's depth completion gave 72% parallel-jaw success ([arXiv 1910.02550](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.02550v2)). TransCG provides 57,715 real RGB-D images under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 ([TransCG](https://github.com/Galaxies99/TransCG/blob/main/README.md)). CoppeliaSim's rasterised depth almost certainly renders the mug as opaque geometry [unverified for CoppeliaSim specifically]. Simulated success on the mug will therefore overstate real performance unless depth dropout is injected on its mask.

## Objaverse-LVIS and procedural slabs fill the gaps OmniObject3D leaves

The thesis's curated OmniObject3D subset yields only two usable pans and few cups, with no trays, cutting boards or salt shakers (thesis repo, internal), even though OmniObject3D itself is CC BY 4.0 with 6,000 scans in 190 categories ([OmniObject3D](https://github.com/omniobject3d/OmniObject3D)). **Objaverse-LVIS** is the only corpus with breadth across all five targets. It contains about 47K objects uniquely assigned to 1,156 LVIS categories, with at most 500 CLIP-proposed candidates per category ([Objaverse, arXiv 2212.08051](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.08051)). The relevant LVIS names are verified: saltshaker, shaker, pepper_mill, mug, cup, teacup, chopping_board, frying_pan, pan_(for_cooking), saucepan, wok, pot, casserole, kettle, teapot, pitcher_(vessel_for_liquid), tray, platter, serving_dish, plate, salad_plate and bowl (mmdet LVIS class list). **Per-category counts remain unknown** because the annotation file could not be fetched. Five lines with `objaverse.load_lvis_annotations()` will produce them, and that should be the first action item after the meeting. Each Objaverse object carries its own CC licence. Across the full corpus, about 721K of roughly 800K objects are CC-BY 4.0 and about 3.5K are CC0 ([HF dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/objaverse)). GraspGen's precedent of keeping only LVIS-matched CC-BY meshes (36,366) is the template for a redistributable subset ([arXiv 2507.13097](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.13097)). Its released Panda grasps on 8,515 of those objects could double as canGraspFrom supervision if our categories are among them [unverified overlap]. **Objaverse++** (ICCVW 2025, ODC-By) adds quality scores and a Transparency flag, which helps select or exclude glass mugs ([ObjaversePlusPlus](https://github.com/TCXX/ObjaversePlusPlus)). Objaverse meshes have no reliable metric scale and arbitrary up-axes, so each must be rescaled to real-world size ranges and re-oriented.

The other corpora serve narrower roles. **ACRONYM** has 17.7M parallel-jaw grasps on 8,872 ShapeNetSem meshes, but its data is CC BY-NC 4.0 and the meshes must be fetched separately ([NVlabs/acronym](https://github.com/NVlabs/acronym)). **YCB** has 77 physical objects including a mug, bowl, plate and a skillet with glass lid, one instance each, which makes it good for real evaluation and useless for variation ([YCB](https://www.ycbbenchmarks.com/object-set/)) [model licence unverified]. **Google Scanned Objects** has over 1,000 scans under Creative Commons ([arXiv 2204.11918](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.11918v1)). **HANDAL** has handle-affordance masks on 212 real tools but is CC-BY-NC-SA ([NVlabs/HANDAL](https://github.com/NVlabs/HANDAL)). **PartNet** has Mug (212), Bowl (208) and Knife (384) with hierarchical parts under ShapeNet terms, a ready source for mug handle/body labels ([arXiv 1812.02713](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.02713)). **RoboCasa** has 2,509 kitchen assets in 153 categories, mostly Luma-generated ([arXiv 2406.02523](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.02523)) [licence unverified].

Trays, boards, salt shakers and plates are geometrically simple, so procedural generation is a legitimate, licence-free supplement. Parametric slabs with rims and handles, and capped cylinders, come with labels by construction. The transfer evidence is encouraging. Tobin et al. trained on millions of procedural objects and reached over 90% simulated success on unseen realistic ones ([arXiv 1710.06425](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.06425)). Bousmalis et al. found procedural shapes slightly beat ShapeNet when mixed with 10% real data ([arXiv 1709.07857](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.07857)). A PS-CNN follow-up reports 95% success on unseen objects *in CoppeliaSim* using primitive abstraction ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/383967956_Multi-Modal_Robust_Geometry_Primitive_Shape_Scene_Abstraction_for_Grasp_Detection)). Superquadric recovery offers a bridge from perceived clouds to member boxes. EMS (CVPR 2022) fits multiple superquadrics robustly ([CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Liu_Robust_and_Accurate_Superquadric_Recovery_A_Probabilistic_Approach_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf)), and Hidden Superquadrics (RSS 2023) derives antipodal grasps per superquadric without learning ([RSS 2023](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss19/p042.html)). **EGAD** (RA-L 2020, BSD-3; over 2,000 evolved shapes plus 49 printable test objects) is generic grasp-difficulty material, not kitchenware ([arXiv 2003.01314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.01314v3)).

For rendering, CoppeliaSim already supplies everything the demo needs. `sim.getVisionSensorDepth` returns metric depth when option bit 0 is set ([manual](https://manual.coppeliarobotics.com/en/regularApi/simGetVisionSensorDepth.htm)), `sim.getVisionSensorImg` returns RGB ([manual](https://manual.coppeliarobotics.com/en/regularApi/simGetVisionSensorImg.htm)), and the "color coded handles" render mode yields per-object instance masks that respect occlusion ([vision sensor dialog](https://manual.coppeliarobotics.com/en/visionSensorPropertiesDialog.htm); [forum](https://forum.coppeliarobotics.com/viewtopic.php?p=40673)). These manual facts come from search snippets. Because each member in the .ttt is its own shape, the colour-coded mode should produce **member-level masks for free** (our inference). Two co-located sensors, one RGB-D and one colour-coded, give aligned labels, and anti-aliasing must be off. For photorealism, CoppeliaSim exports glTF 2.0 for Blender ([import/export](https://manual.coppeliarobotics.com/en/importExport.htm)). From there, **BlenderProc2** (GPL-3.0 code; RGB, depth, normals and segmentation; COCO/BOP/HDF5 output) is the natural renderer ([DLR-RM/BlenderProc](https://github.com/DLR-RM/BlenderProc)), with **Kubric** (Apache-2.0) as an alternative ([google-research/kubric](https://github.com/google-research/kubric)). How faithfully glass materials survive the glTF round trip is [unverified].

## Ranked shortlists for the demo and the paper

The demo shortlist prioritises things that run on the existing scene within three weeks and produce numbers comparable to UTAMP-2's 0.98 F1 and 90%/100% success.

| Rank | (a) 2–3 week integration demo | Why this rank |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Geometric predicate generator** over the .ttt members: box faces + "inside", convex-hull stable faces, plane/area tests, footprint-in-region for isP2PSupportive, face-pair width ≤ 80 mm for canGraspFrom | Needs no training. Directly diffable against UTAMP-2-GT and the OSF PDDL. Addresses the paper's failure mode (missing support facts) |
| 2 | **CoppeliaSim perception path**: metric depth + colour-coded member masks → per-member clouds → OBBs → same rules | Replaces GT geometry with perceived geometry at near-zero cost. Separates perception error from rule error |
| 3 | **GraspGen** (Panda model) on per-object masked clouds, binned to (member, palm, f1, f2) | Verified Panda support, active 2026 maintenance, object-centric input matches the masks. Swap in Contact-GraspNet if the CUDA/PTv3 constraints bite |
| 4 | **canLift** = CoppeliaSim mass (or category lookup) with ±interval and a lever-arm torque check per member | Captures the pan edge-grasp case. Honest baseline any learned estimator must beat |
| 5 | **Evaluation harness** on the 127 configurations: predicate P/R/F1 vs GT, Fast Downward success, plus a noise-injection sweep | Turns the demo into the first experiment of the paper |

A plausible schedule is one week for rules on ground-truth geometry and diffing against the OSF PDDL, one week for the perceived-geometry path and GraspGen binning, and one week for the 127-configuration evaluation and noise sweep. The schedule depends on obtaining the benchmark configurations and UTAMP-2's ground-truth predicate assignments from the supervisors, since no code is public.

The paper shortlist ranks by novelty against the gaps above, multiplied by how directly each item builds on existing assets: the thesis student, the authored members and the UTAMP-2 benchmark.

| Rank | (b) Publishable contribution | Gap it fills |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Face-level predicate student distilled from a VLM teacher with physics-validated labels**: extend the DINOv2 + MLP pairwise student to (obj, part) and (part2, obj2, part1, obj1) tuples. Label with o4-mini/GPT-5-mini plus geometric rules, confirm with CoppeliaSim place-and-settle rollouts, and train on Objaverse-LVIS CC-BY plus procedural slabs | No published VLM→student predicate distillation for TAMP, and no learned pairwise face-support classifier. Targets UTAMP-2-VLM's 10% gap and VLM latency |
| 2 | **Predicate-error → plan-success study with probabilistic grounding**: inject false negatives and false positives per predicate type on the 127 configurations, then compare deterministic grounding with Azran-style conformant grounding and with geometric verification | No controlled noise sweep for manipulation PDDL. Explains the 0.98 F1 / 90% paradox |
| 3 | **Automatic member decomposition**: PartField/Find3D/VLPart offline on meshes → parent-aligned member boxes, scored against the supervisors' authored members and by downstream plan success | Removes UTAMP-2's per-object authoring step. Ground truth already exists in the .ttt |
| 4 | **Detector-to-symbol grasp binning** (GraspGen/Contact-GraspNet → canGraspFrom) evaluated against AGFP-Net-style discrete grasp-type feasibility | Face-level palm/finger predicates are unclaimed, but the step is incremental |
| 5 | **Uncertainty-aware canLift** (VLM material → density × volume interval, torque test) | Unclaimed but small. Better as a component of #1 than as a paper |

Items 1 and 2 form one paper. The student is the method, the noise study is the explanation of why it matters, and the 127-configuration benchmark gives a head-to-head with UTAMP-2-GT and UTAMP-2-VLM. A useful question for October 14 is whether UTAMP-2's ground-truth affordance rules can serve as a label source, since that would let the student be trained against GT, teacher and physics labels separately.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the task. In simulation, perception is not the bottleneck for UTAMP-2: members are authored, meshes and poses are exact, and the hard part is deciding face-level support and grasp facts consistently. Inconsistency, not average accuracy, is what cost the VLM variant 10% of plans. A front end therefore earns its place by being *verifiable*. Geometry gives a ground-truth-grade baseline, learned detectors and VLMs propose, and physics rollouts adjudicate. The learned component becomes publishable once it is framed as cheap, consistent, distilled grounding whose errors are measured by their planning consequences, not by F1 alone.

Three facts should be settled before committing. The first is the Objaverse-LVIS counts for the verified category names, a five-line script. The second is whether GraspGen's PTv3 build runs on the lab GPU. The third is access to the 127 benchmark configurations and UTAMP-2's ground-truth predicate rules. If Objaverse proves thin for trays, boards and shakers, procedural generation covers them with labels by construction. That would make the dataset less of a risk than it looked after OmniObject3D.
